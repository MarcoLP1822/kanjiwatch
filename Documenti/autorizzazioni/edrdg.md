Dear Dr Breen,

I am an independent developer working on Kanji Watch, a standalone Apple Watch app for
reviewing the 2,136 jōyō kanji. It works entirely offline, and I plan to publish it on
the App Store with an optional paid subscription.

The app bundles data derived from KANJIDIC2 (readings, meanings and school grades) and
JMdict (one example word per kanji, with its reading). I follow the Group's licence as
follows:

- a screen named "Sources", reachable from the app's settings menu, acknowledges the
  files, names the EDRDG as their owner, and links to the KANJIDIC and JMdict-EDICT
  project pages and to the licence;
- the same acknowledgement appears in the project documentation and will appear in the
  App Store description;
- the generated data files remain under CC BY-SA 4.0;
- a script re-downloads the current KANJIDIC2 and JMdict and regenerates the data at
  every release of the app, so the versions never go stale.

I am writing about one point I could not settle by reading the licence. Apps sold
through the App Store are wrapped in Apple's FairPlay DRM, while section 2(a)(5)(B) of
CC BY-SA 4.0 forbids applying effective technological measures to the licensed
material. Creative Commons themselves note that distributing CC-licensed material
through such stores may conflict with the licence, and parallel distribution was not
adopted in version 4.0.

May I have your written permission to distribute the derived data inside the app
through the Apple App Store, notwithstanding the technological measures Apple applies
to every app bundle? The derived files themselves carry no DRM, and I am glad to send
them to you, or to publish them separately under CC BY-SA, if that helps.

If any part of my acknowledgement should be worded differently, please tell me and I
will change it. Should the app ever earn anything, I intend to donate to the Group, as
the licence page suggests.

Thank you for the dictionaries. This app would not exist without them.

Kind regards,
Marco Luigi Palma
